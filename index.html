About Me

I’m a U.S. Navy veteran and cybersecurity student at Robert Morris University. My time in the Navy’s Nuclear Reactor Department taught me to work carefully, troubleshoot under pressure, and treat documentation, procedures, and accountability as part of the job. Those habits continue to shape how I approach technical and security problems.

Since starting college, I’ve been building experience across cybersecurity and artificial intelligence. During my internship at Brixmor Property Group as a Cybersecurity Intern, I gained hands-on experience with security ticket triage, vulnerability management, domain assessments, and AI policy alignment using the NIST AI RMF. I’m currently an Artificial Intelligence Intern at O.F. Mossberg & Sons, where I’m contributing to applied AI initiatives and building practical experience.

Outside of internships, I build security tools and research projects to understand systems by working with them directly. My current ReactorBench-LM project explores how a small Transformer trained from scratch learns from a fictional event environment. I’m especially interested in the space where technical systems, security controls, and responsible AI decisions meet.

ReactorBench-LM

View Code

Transformer from scratch · Research in progress

I’m building a small decoder-only Transformer from random weights to study how it learns from event narratives in Aster Station, a fictional synthetic environment.

The project covers data generation, a project-trained tokenizer, model training, and evaluation—without pretrained weights or hosted LLMs.

What I’m working on

  • Improving structured predictions and generalization.
  • Testing balanced sampling and task-weighted training.
  • Preserving failed experiments and reproducible results.
PythonPyTorchSentencePiece PydanticSafetensors

Model-quality checks are still in progress; this is not a deployment-ready model. All training scenarios are fictional, with no real plant records or Navy material.
